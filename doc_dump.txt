Team Name - Junction, this team is for SIH 2026Selected PS : Al-Powered Automatic Block Planning to Maximize Asset Availability for Train Operations on Indian Railways

Problem Statement ID >> SIH26027

PS description - Background: Railway maintenance for fixed infrastructure of Engineering, Traction Distribution, and Signal & Telecommunication departments is currently planned independently. Each department requests maintenance blocks/disconnections via the BDMS system. This planning process is decentralized and manual. This often leads to inefficient block utilization, poor coordination, and suboptimal scheduling,which may reduce asset availability and impact train operations. Detailed Description: Maintenance data-such as defects and overdue tasksâ€”is maintained separately in systems like Track Management System (TMS), Signalling Maintenance & Management System (SMMS), and Traction Distribution Management System (TDMS). Meanwhile, the Control Office Application (COA) manages block corridor availability. Without integration and coordinated scheduling, maintenance blocks/disconnections are not optimally planned, resulting in asset downtime and reduced availability of fixed infrastructure for train operation.Your task is to develop an Automatic Block Planning system that integrates maintenance, defects and corridor data to generate optimized block schedules. The system should prioritize maintenance activities to minimize asset downtime and maximize the availability of critical infrastructure, ensuring uninterrupted train operations. Expected Solution: Participants should build an Al system that includes:

1. Integration of maintenance data (defects, overdue maintenance) from TMS, SMMS, and TDMS with corridor block and block availability as per the Train Time Table and the goods trains forecast from the Control Office.

2. Uses AI/ML algorithms to prioritize and schedule maintenance tasks based on criticality, urgency, and impact on asset availability.

3. Optimize block scheduling to maximize asset uptime by minimizing downtime and efficiently coordinating multi-department activities.

4. Provides block plans over multiple time horizons-weekly and monthlyâ€”to support both short-term and long-term maintenance.

The solution should transform current decentralized and manual block planning into a data-driven, coordinated process that maximizes asset availability, improves safety, and supports reliable train operations.

2. THE ACTUAL PROBLEM

The railway ecosystem does not have one simple pipeline where:

Asset fails → AI predicts it → maintenance happens.

The actual process is much more complicated.

There are multiple:

railway assets

departments

inspection systems

maintenance systems

train movements

block requests

operational restrictions

track alternatives

safety constraints

maintenance priorities

and these interact with each other.

The fundamental problem you discovered is:

Maintenance decisions are made using fragmented asset-condition information, while block decisions must simultaneously account for train operations and requests from multiple railway departments.

Therefore, the objective is not simply predicting failure.

It is:

Predict maintenance requirements early enough to plan the required block at the best possible time while minimizing disruption to train operations and maximizing asset availability.

Q. PRIORITY FINDING — IMPORTANT UNCERTAINTY

Your field findings contained an important discrepancy.

One discussion suggested:

Engineering may receive priority before S&T in certain situations.

Another operational discussion indicated:

Departments aren't necessarily given an inherent blanket preference and parallel coordination can occur.

Do not present either as an absolute railway-wide rule yet.

Your system should therefore initially use:

Configurable priority rules

rather than:

Engineering always wins.

That is a much safer architecture.

75. FINAL PRODUCT POSITIONING

If a judge asks:

"What does your system actually do?"

Your answer should be:

Our system continuously evaluates railway asset condition and maintenance requirements, determines which interventions require operational blocks, combines those requirements with train schedules and block requests from other departments, and uses constraint-based optimization to recommend the safest and least disruptive maintenance window. The final decision remains with the authorized railway controller.

If they ask:

"Where is the AI?"

Say:

AI predicts asset maintenance requirements, risk and potentially work duration. An optimization engine then converts those predictions into an operationally feasible block plan.

If they ask:

"Why not just use an optimization algorithm?"

Say:

Optimization needs to know what maintenance is going to be required. Predictive intelligence provides that forward-looking information, while optimization determines when the intervention should occur.

That distinction is very strong for your SIH presentation.

OHE Job C

   duration = 45 min

S&T Job D

   duration = 30 min

subject to:

Can't overlap certain jobs

Must occur within allowed window

Train movement can't be blocked

Some work can happen in parallel

Some work requires isolation

Alternative track may be available

Certain work has priority

This is exactly the kind of constraint-based scheduling problem OR-Tools is designed for. Google also provides scheduling examples based on start/end times, precedence constraints and non-overlap constraints. (Google for Developers)
